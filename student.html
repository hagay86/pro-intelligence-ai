MK
Can you remind me what Mr. Chen said about u-substitution vs. integration by parts?
AI
Mr. Chen's rule of thumb (from last Tuesday's lesson):

Use u-substitution when you see a function and its derivative both in the integrand. Pick u as the inner function.

Use integration by parts (LIATE rule) when the integrand is a product of two different "types" of functions — Logarithmic, Inverse trig, Algebraic, Trig, Exponential. The earlier in that list goes as u.

He emphasized 📝 May 7 lecture: "Try u-sub first. If it doesn't simplify, then by parts. Don't jump to by parts because it looks fancier."
MK
Show me an example where by parts is the right choice.
AI
A classic one from 📝 worksheet 4: ∫ x·eˣ dx

Here u-sub fails because there's no obvious inner-and-derivative pair. By parts works perfectly:
• u = x (algebraic — goes first per LIATE)
• dv = eˣ dx
• du = dx, v = eˣ
• ∫ x·eˣ dx = x·eˣ − ∫ eˣ dx = x·eˣ − eˣ + C
