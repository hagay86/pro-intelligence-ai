I'm running a fitness brand on Meta. ROAS is 1.4, CAC is way above target. What would Hagay do?
AI
Hagay's AI grounded in 3 chats
Hagay sees this pattern often — and he's clear about the order to attack it.

First, audit creative, not audience. 1.4 ROAS with above-target CAC almost always means the hook isn't doing work. In 📁 Q4 Meta — fitness pilot he found that 78% of underperforming accounts had the same problem: pretty visuals, weak first 3 seconds.

His diagnostic order:
1. Pull thumbstop ratio per creative. If anything is below 30%, kill it before touching anything else.
2. Look at the breakdown by placement. Hagay caps Audience Network aggressively for fitness — it inflates spend with bot traffic.
3. Only then look at audience. If the top 3 creatives are healthy but ROAS is still bad, then it's targeting.

Want me to walk you through pulling the thumbstop report? I have the exact path Hagay uses.
MK
2:18 PM
Yes please. Also — why does he cap Audience Network for fitness specifically?
AI
Hagay's AI grounded in 1 chat · Aug 2025
Good catch — this came up specifically in a conversation Hagay had with Claude on 📅 Aug 14. He'd been seeing CPMs on Audience Network at half the price of Feed, which felt like a win, but the post-purchase CAC was 3.2x higher. He traced it: AN was driving clicks from incentivized traffic (game ad networks reskinning offers). Fitness offers are especially vulnerable because the "free trial" language gets gamed.

Since then his default is: AN off until proven otherwise, for any health, fitness, or trial-based offer. Other verticals he'll leave it on.

On the thumbstop report — go to Ads Manager → Columns → Customize → add Video Average Play Time and 3-sec Video Plays. Divide 3-sec plays by impressions. Below 30% means people are scrolling past. Want me to draft the message you'd send your client about killing those creatives?
